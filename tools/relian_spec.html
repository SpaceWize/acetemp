Spelling and grammar rules

  • Spell words by sound. Names and acronyms are spelled letter by letter.
  • Long vowels: u + vowel (uo, ue, ua, ui). e = ea or ee. g = gi.
  • a is "ay" after a consonant or at the start, and "ray" after a vowel.
  • Extra-n: en after a vowel sound becomes nen, across word boundaries. y is a consonant and does not trigger it.
  • Letter-name merges: consonant + vowel already in the name merges (ja = jay).
  • Verbs are either active or stative. Active verbs are actions: go, push, eat, live, fight, write, read. Stative verbs are not movement or physical: need, do, want, have. Stative verbs always follow the active verb.
  • Word order: action verb first, then subject, except with i. Noun pairs put the modifier first.
  • Prepositions go before the noun. Conjunctions are particles.
  • Possessive: owner + lay + noun phrase. Plural s' attaches to the singular spelling.
  • Question word first, then active verb, then subject, then any stative verb, then suffixes. The reversed ? goes at the very end, after all endings.
  • Basic word order is verb, subject, object. The object goes at the end of the sentence.
  • A sentence needs an active verb.
  • Adjectives come before the noun. Numbers are Arabic numerals.
  • Words not yet in the vocabulary (want, need, carry, write, eat, read, push, live, fight, articles, with, because) are built with the translation method: English to romanized, then romanized to symbol.
  • Long vowels are two joined glyphs: the u glyph and the vowel glyph. y is a consonant.
  • Word spacing is a space.
  • Capital mark: a short tick at the top-left of the letter's spine marks a capital. It can be added to any letter.
  • Present: ru, sentence-final. Past: ayaru, sentence-final. Future: ruya, sentence-final. Negation: nenayio after a vowel sound (a, e, i, o, u; not y), otherwise enayio. -ing: -engi.
  • Months: first three letters + hyphen + -mon (Jayen-mon = January).

Pronouns

  • I = i (pronounced "ee")
  • You = yio
  • He = heas
  • She = esshea
  • We = wea
  • They = tey
  • It = iot

Question words

  • What = wayt
  • Who = wkio
  • Where = wearar
  • When = weanen
  • Why = wy
  • How = waykio

Punctuation

  • Sentence end: horizontal dash
  • Comma: plus sign
  • Exclamation: inverted triangle with a filled dot above it
  • Colon: two circles joined by a solid line
  • Semicolon: same mark as the colon (confirm)
  • Open quote: hard C facing forward
  • Close quote: hard C facing backward
  • Hyphen: kept in the glyphs as written (Jayen-mon, pronounced the same as spelled); symbol to be drawn

Particles and copula

  • To = tu
  • And = aynendeh
  • Is / was = essea (either tense)
  • Have = shown with the possessive lay mark
